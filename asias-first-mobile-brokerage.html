Mikaal Abdulla on Building Asia's First Mobile-Only Brokerage

📅 September 8, 2026
✎ Mikaal Abdulla
⌚ 3 min read

When Mikaal Abdulla launched 8 Securities as Asia's first mobile-only brokerage, the category did not yet exist in the region. Retail investing in Asia Pacific still ran on desktop platforms and branch relationships, and a brokerage built entirely for the phone was a product bet rather than a proven model.

Abdulla, a fintech executive and startup founder, arrived at that bet with an unusual combination of experience. A decade leading E*TRADE's expansion across 14 markets had shown him how retail investors behave in very different environments. Earlier work in Deloitte's technology consulting practice had shown him how financial products are actually assembled. That background, traced in his career in fintech leadership, explains why the 8 Securities thesis started from the user rather than the technology.

Designing for the Phone, Not Shrinking the Desktop

The defining product decision at 8 Securities was architectural. Instead of compressing a desktop trading terminal onto a smaller screen, the team designed account opening, funding, research, and execution around how people actually use their phones: in short sessions, with limited patience for complexity. Every screen had to earn its place. Each step between download and first trade was treated as a point where a new investor might give up, so the team removed steps rather than adding explanations.

That decision extended below the interface. 8 Securities became the first licensed consumer financial services company in Asia to run fully on the cloud, a choice that reduced infrastructure overhead and shortened the time between an idea and a shipped feature. For a regulated business, that combination was uncommon. It also allowed the product to be updated continuously instead of on the slower release cycles common to incumbent brokerages, which mattered when the goal was to learn quickly from how real customers used the app.

The products that define a market are rarely the most technically complex. They are the ones that solve the right problem for the right user at the right moment.

A Sequence of Regional Firsts

With the mobile foundation in place, 8 Securities introduced a series of products that were new to the region: a robo-advisor for automated portfolio management, social trading that let investors see and learn from one another's activity, and fractional shares that lowered the cost of owning a position. Together they addressed one barrier from several directions: the perception among first-time investors that markets were complicated, expensive, and built for someone else.

Each concept had precedents elsewhere. Deploying them in Asia at scale required conviction about timing and a clear read of local investor needs. Pricing followed the same logic. The company introduced subscription pricing, bringing a Netflix-style model to brokerage, an approach examined in his analysis of subscription pricing. In each case the question was less whether a product existed globally and more whether investors in the region were ready for it, and whether the platform could deliver it within local regulatory requirements.

Recognition and What the Build Proved

Outside validation came from TechCrunch Disrupt, where 8 Securities was a finalist, and from AWS, which named it a Top 5 Startup in Asia. Both reflected the product thesis and the execution behind it. The platform later executed a stock trade end to end through an AI agent, another first in its record. For a regulated startup, that kind of recognition carries practical weight, because credibility with partners, regulators, and customers has to be built alongside the product itself.

For Mikaal Abdulla, mobile-first was less a feature than an operating principle: start from what financial consumers in high-growth markets need, not from what already works elsewhere. That principle explains why the platform's firsts arrived in a deliberate sequence rather than all at once, each building on the trust and habits established by the last. He discusses these product principles in his YouTube video commentary.

Conclusion

The mobile-first brokerage model that 8 Securities helped pioneer in Asia is now standard practice worldwide. The story of how it was built, through cloud infrastructure, mobile-native design, and a run of product firsts, remains a useful reference for anyone building consumer financial products in Asia Pacific.
